Low-Carb Drinks: What to Drink and What to Avoid

Drinks are the hidden source of carbs in most diets. A single glass of orange juice has about 20 grams of carbs, a sweetened coffee can have 40 grams, and a pint of beer has about 15 grams. None of them fills you up. Choosing the right drinks is one of the easiest wins on a low-carb diet, and it starts with knowing what is in the glass.

Water, the best drink

Water is the best drink on any diet. It has no carbs, no calories, and it helps with the headaches and tiredness some people get in the first week of low-carb eating. Aim for six to eight glasses a day, and more if you exercise or the weather is hot.

Sparkling water with lemon, lime, or cucumber is a good alternative if you miss fizzy drinks. Add mint or berries for flavour. Avoid tonic water, which contains sugar, and flavoured waters, which often contain sugar or sweeteners.

Tea and coffee

Tea and coffee are both low in carbs, as long as you drink them without sugar. Black coffee has almost no carbs. Tea, green or black, is also fine. Add cream instead of milk, because cream is lower in carbs and adds richness.

Avoid sweetened coffee drinks, which can contain more carbs than a meal. A large flavoured latte can have 40 grams of carbs. If you like sweet coffee, use a sugar-free sweetener and ask for unsweetened milk alternatives.

Alcohol

Alcohol is a separate issue from carbs. Spirits such as vodka, gin, whisky, and tequila have almost no carbs, but they contain calories and can lower your resolve. Dry wine has about 2 grams of carbs per glass, while sweet wine has much more.

Beer is high in carbs, about 13 grams per pint, and sweet cocktails are worse. If you drink, choose a spirit with soda water or a glass of dry wine, limit yourself to one or two drinks, and eat before you drink so you are less likely to snack afterwards.

Drinks to avoid

Avoid sugary soft drinks, fruit juice, energy drinks, and sweetened teas, which are all high in sugar. Diet soft drinks have no carbs, but they are not a healthy habit, and some people find they increase cravings for sweet food.

Smoothies are also high in carbs, because they concentrate the sugar from several pieces of fruit into one glass. Eat whole fruit instead, and drink water. If you want a flavoured drink, make it yourself with sparkling water and fresh fruit.

Making your own flavoured water

Flavoured water is easy to make at home, and it is far better than a shop-bought flavoured drink. Add slices of lemon, lime, or cucumber to a jug of cold water and leave it in the fridge for an hour. Add mint, basil, or a few berries for more flavour.

Iced tea is another good option: brew tea, let it cool, and serve it over ice with lemon and mint. Do not add sugar. For coffee, try it cold-brewed, which is smoother and less bitter than hot coffee, and serve it with cream. Homemade drinks are cheaper, healthier, and taste better than anything in a bottle.

Frequently Asked Questions

What drinks are low-carb?

Water, sparkling water, unsweetened tea and black coffee. Cream is fine in coffee; milk is higher in carbs.

Can I drink alcohol on a low-carb diet?

Spirits with soda water and dry wine are the lowest-carb choices. Beer and sweet cocktails are high in carbs.

Is diet soda okay on low-carb?

It has no carbs, but it is not a healthy habit, and some people find it increases cravings for sweet food.
