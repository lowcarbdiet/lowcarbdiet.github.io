Low-Carb Bread Alternatives: Wraps, Buns and Flatbreads

Bread is the food most people miss on a low-carb diet. It is the base for sandwiches, the side for soup, and the habit of a lifetime. The good news is that there are several alternatives, and some of them are genuinely good. None of them is exactly bread, but each one does the job bread did, and that is what matters.

Lettuce wraps and cabbage leaves

Large lettuce leaves, such as romaine or butter lettuce, make excellent wraps. They are crisp, fresh, and hold fillings well if you use two leaves and fold carefully. They are best for cold fillings such as tuna, chicken, or cheese.

Cabbage leaves, blanched for a minute, are sturdier and work for hot fillings. Use them for tacos or for wrapping a burger. They are not bread, but they are a practical, fresh alternative that adds vegetables to the meal.

Cloud bread

Cloud bread is made from eggs, cream cheese, and cream of tartar, whisked and baked into light, fluffy rounds. It has almost no carbs and a soft, bread-like texture. It is best eaten fresh, on the day it is made, because it softens quickly.

Use cloud bread for sandwiches, as a base for eggs, or toasted with butter. It is not as sturdy as bread, so keep fillings light. It takes ten minutes to make and uses ingredients you probably have.

Almond flour bread

Almond flour bread is the closest thing to real bread in low-carb cooking. It is made with almond flour, eggs, baking powder, and a little psyllium husk, which gives it structure. It bakes into a loaf that slices well and toasts nicely.

Almond flour bread is denser than wheat bread and higher in calories, so eat smaller slices. It keeps for three days in the fridge and freezes well in slices. It is best for toast and sandwiches, not for eating plain.

Cauliflower flatbreads and other swaps

Cauliflower flatbreads are made from grated cauliflower, egg, and cheese, baked into thin rounds. They work as pizza bases, wraps, and flatbreads. They are softer than bread, but they hold toppings well.

Other swaps include portobello mushrooms as burger buns, aubergine slices as sandwich bread, and cheese crisps as crackers. None of them replaces bread exactly, but each one solves a specific problem, and together they cover most of the jobs bread used to do.

Which alternative for which job

Different alternatives suit different jobs. For a sandwich, almond flour bread is the closest to the real thing, because it slices and toasts well. For a wrap, lettuce leaves are best, because they are fresh and crisp. For a pizza base, a cauliflower flatbread holds toppings well. For a burger, a portobello mushroom or a lettuce wrap both work.

For toast, cloud bread is quick to make, while almond flour bread is sturdier. For crackers, cheese crisps are the simplest option. Match the alternative to the job, and do not expect one alternative to do everything. A small collection of alternatives, each used for what it does best, covers most of the jobs bread used to do.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best low-carb bread substitute?

Almond flour bread is closest to real bread. Lettuce wraps are best for cold fillings, and cloud bread is quickest to make.

Is cloud bread really low-carb?

Yes. Cloud bread is made from eggs and cream cheese, so it has almost no carbs. Eat it fresh, as it softens quickly.

Can I buy low-carb bread in shops?

Some shops sell it, but check the label. Some low-carb breads are high in fibre and still contain significant carbs.
