Eating Out Low-Carb: Restaurants, Cafes and Fast Food

Eating out is the moment most low-carb diets fail. The menu is full of bread, rice, pasta, and potatoes, the portions are large, and it feels awkward to ask for changes. But eating out on low-carb is easier than it looks. Almost every restaurant can make a low-carb meal if you know what to ask for, and the swaps are simple.

The basic strategy

Order a protein and vegetables, and skip the starch. A grilled chicken breast with salad, a steak with green beans, or a fish fillet with roasted vegetables are all low-carb meals that most restaurants serve. Ask for the sauce on the side, because sauces often contain sugar or flour.

Replace bread, rice, pasta, and potatoes with extra vegetables or a salad. Most restaurants will do this without charging extra, and many now offer cauliflower rice or courgette noodles as alternatives. Do not be shy about asking; it is a normal request.

Cuisine by cuisine

At an Italian restaurant, order a salad and a grilled meat or fish dish, and skip the pasta and bread. At an Indian restaurant, choose a tandoori dish or a curry without rice or naan, and ask for extra vegetables. At a Chinese restaurant, choose a stir-fry without rice or noodles, and ask for the sauce on the side.

At a Mexican restaurant, order fajitas without tortillas, or a salad bowl with meat, guacamole, and cheese. At a burger place, order a bunless burger with salad. At a cafe, order eggs and skip the toast, or a salad with chicken.

Drinks

Water is the best choice. Sparkling water with lemon is a good alternative to a soft drink. Unsweetened tea and black coffee are fine. Avoid sugary drinks, fruit juice, and sweetened coffee drinks, which can contain more carbs than a meal.

Alcohol is a separate issue. Spirits such as vodka, gin, and whisky have almost no carbs, but they still contain calories and can lower your resolve. Beer is high in carbs, and sweet cocktails are worse. If you drink, choose a spirit with soda water and limit yourself to one or two.

Fast food

Fast food is possible on low-carb if you choose carefully. A bunless burger with salad, grilled chicken without the bun, or a salad bowl are all reasonable options. Avoid fries, breaded chicken, and sugary sauces.

Check the nutrition information, which most chains publish online. A single meal can contain a day's worth of carbs if you are not careful, but it can also be a perfectly good low-carb meal if you order wisely. Plan before you go, and do not let hunger make the decision.

Breakfast out on low-carb

Breakfast out is easier than it looks on low-carb. A hotel buffet usually offers eggs, cheese, yoghurt, and fruit, and you can skip the bread, cereal, and pastries. Ask for eggs cooked to order if they are not on the buffet, and choose full-fat yoghurt with berries instead of a sweetened one.

At a cafe, order an omelette or scrambled eggs and ask for extra vegetables or avocado instead of toast. A black coffee or a coffee with cream is a better choice than a sweetened latte. If the menu is limited, a simple plate of eggs and a side salad is a good breakfast, and it will keep you full until lunch.

Frequently Asked Questions

What should I order at a restaurant on low-carb?

A grilled protein with vegetables or salad, sauce on the side, and no bread, rice, pasta or potatoes. Ask for extra vegetables instead.

Can I eat fast food on a low-carb diet?

Yes, if you choose carefully: a bunless burger, grilled chicken without the bun, or a salad bowl. Avoid fries and breaded items.

What drinks are low-carb?

Water, sparkling water, unsweetened tea and black coffee. Avoid sugary drinks, juice and sweet cocktails.
