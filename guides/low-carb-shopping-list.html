Low-Carb Shopping List: What to Buy Every Week

A good shopping list is the difference between a week of easy low-carb eating and a week of improvising. When the fridge is full of the right foods, cooking is quick and choices are easy. When it is not, you end up ordering a takeaway. This list covers the staples to buy every week, the fresh foods to choose, and the items to leave on the shelf.

The weekly staples

Buy eggs, butter, olive oil, cheese, and nuts every week. These are the foundation of low-carb cooking, and they keep well. Add cream, Greek yoghurt, and mayonnaise if you use them. Keep salt, pepper, herbs, and spices stocked, because they make simple food taste good.

Buy a few tins of tuna, a jar of olives, and some pickles for quick lunches. Keep almond flour and a sugar-free sweetener in the cupboard for baking. These staples mean you can always make a meal, even if the fridge is nearly empty.

Fresh foods

Buy meat and fish for the week, choosing a mix of chicken, beef, pork, and fish. Buy eggs by the dozen. Choose vegetables that keep well, such as broccoli, cauliflower, courgettes, peppers, and leafy greens, and buy berries for the week.

Buy avocados, which are a good source of healthy fats and fibre. Buy lemons and limes for dressings and drinks. Avoid buying bread, rice, pasta, and potatoes, because if they are in the house, you will eat them.

Reading labels

Read the label on anything in a packet. Check the total carbs, the fibre, and the sugar. Net carbs are total carbs minus fibre. Be careful with sauces, dressings, and processed meats, which often contain added sugar or starch.

Avoid foods with a long list of ingredients you do not recognise. A short ingredient list is usually a good sign. And remember that low-fat products often have added sugar to replace the fat, so choose full-fat versions.

Shopping tips

Shop with a list and stick to it. Do not shop when you are hungry, because you will buy more than you need. Shop the edges of the supermarket first, where the fresh meat, fish, and vegetables are, and spend less time in the middle aisles, where the processed foods live.

Buy frozen vegetables and berries, which are as nutritious as fresh and keep for months. Buy meat in bulk when it is on offer and freeze it in portions. A well-stocked freezer is a low-carb cook's best friend.

Shopping by section

Shop by section, and you will buy better food. Start in the produce section, and fill your basket with vegetables and berries. Move to the meat and fish counter, and choose a mix of chicken, beef, pork, and fish. Then go to dairy, for cheese, cream, and yoghurt. Finish in the frozen section, for vegetables and berries that keep for months.

Spend as little time as possible in the middle aisles, where the bread, biscuits, and ready meals live. If you do go there, go with a list and a purpose: olive oil, nuts, and spices. A shop that follows the sections is a shop that fills the fridge with real food.

Frequently Asked Questions

What should I always have in a low-carb kitchen?

Eggs, butter, olive oil, cheese, nuts, cream, and a good supply of vegetables and meat. These cover most meals.

Are frozen vegetables okay on low-carb?

Yes. Frozen vegetables are as nutritious as fresh, keep for months, and are often cheaper.

How do I avoid buying high-carb foods?

Shop with a list, avoid shopping when hungry, and spend most of your time in the fresh food aisles, not the middle of the shop.
