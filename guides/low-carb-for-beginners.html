Low-Carb for Beginners: Your First 7 Days

The first week of low-carb eating is the hardest, and it is also the most important. Your body is adjusting to a new way of eating, your kitchen is full of foods you are not supposed to eat, and everyone around you is eating bread. This guide walks you through the first seven days, day by day, so you know what to expect and what to do.

Before you start

Clear the kitchen of the biggest temptations: biscuits, crisps, sugary drinks, and bread. You do not need to throw everything away, but put it out of sight, in a high cupboard, or give it away. Stock the fridge with eggs, cheese, vegetables, and meat, so the easy option is a good option.

Plan your first week of meals in advance, and shop for them in one trip. If you take medicine for diabetes or blood pressure, talk to your doctor before you start, because your doses may need adjusting as your blood sugar falls.

Days 1 to 3

Days one to three are the hardest. You may feel hungry, tired, or irritable, and you may crave bread and sugar. This is normal. Your body is used to running on glucose, and it takes a few days to adjust.

Drink plenty of water, eat enough salt, and do not cut calories too hard. Eat three meals a day with protein and vegetables, and have a low-carb snack ready if you get hungry. Go to bed early, because tiredness makes cravings worse.

Days 4 to 7

By day four or five, most people feel better. Energy returns, hunger settles, and the cravings ease. You may notice that you are less hungry between meals, and that your clothes feel looser, partly because your body is holding less water.

Use this week to learn what works. Notice which meals keep you full, which snacks you enjoy, and which situations are hardest. Keep a simple food diary for the first week, noting what you ate and how you felt. It will help you adjust the plan to your life.

After the first week

After the first week, low-carb eating gets easier. You know what to eat, you have recipes you like, and your body has adjusted. Now is the time to think about the long term: how to eat out, how to handle holidays, and how to keep the diet interesting.

Do not aim for perfection. If you eat something high in carbs, do not give up. One meal does not ruin a diet, and the next meal is a fresh start. The goal is a pattern of eating that you can sustain, not a perfect week.

Your first shopping trip

Your first low-carb shopping trip needs a list, or you will come home with the wrong things. Buy eggs, butter, olive oil, cheese, and nuts first, because these are the staples. Then buy meat and fish for the week, and vegetables such as broccoli, cauliflower, courgettes, and leafy greens. Add berries, avocados, and lemons.

Do not buy bread, rice, pasta, potatoes, or sugary drinks, because if they are in the house, you will eat them. Buy cream instead of milk for your coffee, and full-fat yoghurt instead of low-fat. One good shop, with a clear list, sets up your first week.

Frequently Asked Questions

What should I eat in my first week of low-carb?

Eggs, meat, fish, vegetables, cheese and nuts. Remove bread, rice, pasta, potatoes and sugary drinks first.

Why do I feel tired in the first few days?

Your body is adjusting to fewer carbs. Drink water, eat enough salt, and do not cut calories too hard at the same time.

Should I count carbs from day one?

It helps to track for the first week so you learn which foods are high in carbs. After that, many people manage without strict counting.
